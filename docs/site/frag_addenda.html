Theory

Addenda to the first paper's theory

The paper (25 November) presents version 2 as a small number of addenda to the postulates of Singaravadivelan and Langley (2026). Every assumption of that theory is kept: experiences of elements in relations, primitives and composites, content and context descriptions, and two linked Cobweb taxonomies in which a composite names each of its two parts by a context concept.

Addenda for version 2

Parsing considers every way to segment an experience into chunks and weighs each candidate chunk by the share of analyses that contain it. The first theory built one partonomic tree from the bottom up, adding the best recognized chunk until none cleared a threshold, so a wrong early choice could not be undone. Version 2 scores every span with inside and outside passes and reports the tree with the most expected correct chunks (Parsing).

Generation writes an experience one element at a time, and each choice considers every chunk that could surround the current position. The first theory expanded a composite from the top down. Version 2 fills positions in order, drawing each element from the counts at its context concept, weighted by the outside score of its position (Generation).

Learning stores every candidate chunk in both taxonomies, weighted by its posterior probability, and the system parses each experience with the memory built from those before it. The first theory learned from sentences paired with parse trees. Version 2 removes the trees: parsing already proposes the candidates, and learning treats each one as an instance with its posterior as a count (Learning).

Addenda for the memory studies

Short-term memory holds a small number of elements, each of which may be a chunk. Recognizing a familiar chunk lets one slot hold many primitives. The number of slots is seven, or four following Cowan, and it is never fit to an experiment.

The familiarity of an experience is the stored weight of the chunks that span it. This needs no new machinery, because learning already stores each chunk with its posterior weight. It is the score in the segmentation and grammar-learning studies (Psychology).

Recall of a damaged chunk uses its concept and its surviving parts as cues. This is Cobweb's flexible prediction applied to memory: the system searches the stored chunks under the same concept for one that agrees with the parts that survive. It is the recall rule of the digit-span study.
