# r229: is the chess realism judge file-symmetric?

Hypothesis (paper, chess paragraph): the piece-square classifier of `r220_judge.py` (tau = .702) is nearly file-symmetric, so a real
position moved sideways still scores "real"; the same symmetry would explain why r227's where-file redraws (which move a chunk to
another file) stay "real" at a high rate.

Test: the 1,000 real held-out positions of the judge's own validation control (`train_long.fen` lines [25459:26459], 95.7% "real" with
no change). Each transform is applied to every position it fits on the board, the result is kept if python-chess `is_valid()` is true,
and the judge classifies it. Code `experiments/v3/diag/r229_judge_file_symmetry.py`; data `r229_judge_file_symmetry.json`; one run on a
compute node, no randomness.

| transform | positions it fits | legal kept | scored real | share real of kept | mean score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none (control) | 1000 | 1000 | 957 | .957 | .950 |
| mirror a<->h | 1000 | 1000 | 789 | .789 | .818 |
| shift +1 file | 26 | 26 | 16 | .615 | .717 |
| shift -1 file | 53 | 53 | 38 | .717 | .781 |
| shift +2 files | 4 | 4 | 2 | .500 | .669 |
| shift -2 files | 13 | 13 | 5 | .385 | .433 |
| shifts +/-1 pooled | 79 | 79 | 54 | .684 | |
| shifts +/-2 pooled | 17 | 17 | 7 | .412 | |

Every legal transform stays legal (the kept count equals the fit count). A shift fits few positions because a middle-game position
usually has pieces on both the a-file and the h-file side; the shift rows rest on 4 to 53 positions each (the +/-2 rows on 4 and 13) and are only indicative.
The mirror uses all 1,000 positions and is the clean test.

Reference controls from `r220_judge.json`: random legal positions score real .03 (pieces shuffled) and .006 (random play).
r227 (same judge, positions generated by the learner, N = 4000, mean of seeds 13 and 17): identity .945 real; where-file kc=0 .890;
where-file kc=99 .681 real.

## Verdict: refuted as stated

The judge is not file-symmetric. Mirroring costs 17 points of "real" (.957 -> .789; mean score .950 -> .818), and the shifted positions lose 27 to 34 points at one file
(.615 to .717 against .957) and more at two files (.41 pooled). It is also far from file-blind: shifted and mirrored real positions still pass
at .4 to .8, against .03 for random legal boards, so the judge accepts much of what a file move leaves (pawn structure, piece counts, material)
and penalizes the part that depends on the file of each piece (the 768 piece-square indicators, such as king and rook files).
The where-file redraw rate (.681 real at kc=99) lies inside this range (.684 for the pooled one-file shifts, .789 for the mirror), so the judge's partial file tolerance accounts for the
high "real" share of where-file output, and the hypothesis that the judge is nearly symmetric is not needed for that.
The link to shifted-position compression (a shape-based coder handles shifted boards because shapes do not name files) is not touched by this test.
