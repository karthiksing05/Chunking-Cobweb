# Weigh Every Chunk Before TRELLIS Commits

TRELLIS v2 can add all five capabilities without dropping a pillar if Cobweb stays the only knowledge source and the parser stops committing greedily. In that design, every candidate composition becomes a chart item scored by both hierarchies. An inside pass builds the user's "frontier of valid parses going up", and an outside pass turns the scores into chunk posteriors, which are the "strength" the user wants. A minimum-Bayes-risk (MBR) decode then picks the best non-crossing set of spans "going down"; any span with posterior above 0.5 is guaranteed to cross no other and so marks a "confirmed" chunk. Better search alone will not pay. v1's beam lost at every width, and inside-outside trained without brackets recovered 37% of ATIS brackets against 90% with them ([Pereira & Schabes 1992](https://aclanthology.org/P92-1017/)). The chart must therefore score candidates with CCM-style odds (constituent versus distituent, i.e. non-constituent) rather than v1's raw log-probability sums. The same outside pass is the principled source of multi-level chunk context. Each level must enter as its own attribute at several granularities, because abstracted context more than doubled v1's omissions on the 39-word lexicon. For unsupervised learning, bits answer both "what makes a chunk valuable" and "how few chunks". In Goldsmith's propose-then-evaluate scheme, chart posteriors propose and a description-length gain decides, so chunk quality and chunk count become two terms of one sum, kept incremental by prequential codelength computed from Cobweb's own counts. The domain plan should de-risk on synthetic and real Chinese-character grammars, then move to chess, whose recall literature already scores reconstruction in omission and commission, with the Penn Treebank as a staged benchmark throughout. Five v1-era rules (stay greedy, never feed parser output back, generation locked, no hints, one parameter set) conflict with this plan and need the user's explicit re-scoping before work starts.

## v1 omits 6% of large-grammar substructures, and its search fixes failed for a diagnosable reason

TRELLIS v1 is close to a literal build of the "probabilistic concept hierarchies" framework in Langley's essay ([Langley 2025](http://www.cogsys.org/proceedings/2025/paper-2025-3.pdf); [Singaravadivelan & Langley 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30414)). Every element carries two descriptions, each sorted through its own Cobweb taxonomy: a content description (its parts) and a context description (its surroundings). Parsing is greedy bottom-up recognition, generation is top-down recall, and learning is plain Cobweb incorporation of gold unlabeled parse trees. Following GRIDS, an omission error is a held-out gold substructure (unlabeled bracket) that TRELLIS's parse misses, and a commission error is a generated sentence the target grammar rejects ([Langley & Stromsten 2000](http://www.isle.org/~langley/papers/grids.ecml2k.pdf); [main.tex](../confs/acs-26/paper/main.tex)). The paper's figures come from 20-seed runs. Their endpoints at 300 training sentences are tabulated below. The "Table 1" values in the memory notes do not match these CSVs, so the CSVs should be treated as the reference ([RESULTS.md](../confs/acs-26/RESULTS.md); [large CSV](../confs/acs-26/grammar_experiment_20seed/large/aggregated.csv); [term_high CSV](../confs/acs-26/terminal_experiment_20seed/high/aggregated.csv)).

| Condition (20 seeds, n = 300) | Omission: held-out substructures missed | Commission: generations the target grammar rejects |
|---|---|---|
| small (3 nonterminals) | 0.04% ± 0.2 | 0.0% |
| med (6 nonterminals) | 3.3% ± 1.6 | 1.1% ± 1.0 |
| large (8 nonterminals) | **6.2% ± 1.9** | 1.6% ± 1.0 |
| term_low (11-word lexicon) | 5.7% ± 4.7 | **6.2% ± 5.3** |
| term_med (22 words) | 7.0% ± 6.2 | 4.7% ± 1.9 |
| term_high (39 words) | **9.7% ± 3.2** | 3.4% ± 1.6 |

One structural fact shapes v2. The parser always returns one complete binary tree with n − 1 substructures, the same number as the gold tree. Missed and spurious substructures are therefore equal in number, so on the parse side omission and commission coincide. The only independent evidence of commission comes from generation. A chart decoder that may return partial parses would separate the two for the first time, which is what GRIDS-style reporting needs.

The record of what moved these numbers points one way: **every durable gain came from representation or from the ranker's objective, never from search.** Making child complexity visible in the content instance cut omissions from 15% to 10% on the large grammar and from 23% to 9% on term_high ([faithful representation push](/Users/karthiksing05/.claude/projects/-Users-karthiksing05-Documents-ISLE-Research-ChunkingCobweb/memory/project_faithful_representation_push.md)). The class_lp ranker raised the step-pick rate along gold trajectories from 83.4% to 98.3%, and cut medium-grammar omissions from 14% to 3% ([class_lp notes](/Users/karthiksing05/.claude/projects/-Users-karthiksing05-Documents-ISLE-Research-ChunkingCobweb/memory/project_class_lp_heuristic.md)). Every search change lost. A beam over merge sequences did worse at every width. A supervised step ranker trained on gold frontiers still left 16% of substructures omitted, because the prefixes it built itself differed from gold ones. A hard production-validity gate collapsed parse accuracy to 0.30 ([representation push](/Users/karthiksing05/.claude/projects/-Users-karthiksing05-Documents-ISLE-Research-ChunkingCobweb/memory/project_acs26_f1_representation_push.md); [CKY notes](/Users/karthiksing05/.claude/projects/-Users-karthiksing05-Documents-ISLE-Research-ChunkingCobweb/memory/project_cky_induced_grammar.md)). The one global method that worked was CKY over a grammar whose symbols were context ancestors at a fixed depth. It reached 0.973 parse accuracy on the medium grammar, kept about 99% of gold brackets valid, and made about 77% of greedy's wrong brackets impossible. It was nonetheless set aside as "straying too far" ([stay-greedy rule](/Users/karthiksing05/.claude/projects/-Users-karthiksing05-Documents-ISLE-Research-ChunkingCobweb/memory/feedback_stay_greedy_clean_pos.md)). Its code is missing from the repository and from git history, so those numbers cannot be re-verified.

The literature reads these failures as model error rather than search error. When neural translation was decoded exactly, the model's single best output was the empty translation for more than half of all sentences ([Stahlberg & Byrne 2019](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1331/)). Wider search exposes a mis-specified objective instead of repairing it. v1's beam fits that pattern: its additive score penalizes the rare, low-probability modifier chunks that gold trees contain. Two further problems compound this. Locally normalized scores cannot compare competing partial analyses on equal terms ([Andor et al. 2016](https://aclanthology.org/P16-1231/)). And a ranker trained only on gold prefixes suffers the exposure bias that dynamic oracles were designed to remove ([Goldberg & Nivre 2013](https://aclanthology.org/Q13-1033/)). One caveat weakens v1's own evidence. The beam scored states with the `context_forward` formula, not with class_lp, so "the beam is worse" was never tested with the ranker that actually shipped ([parse_mh.py](../src/parse_mh.py)).

Some of v1's remaining errors are the kind a chart fixes, and some are not. Greedy commitments such as merging the verb–determiner pair "found the" cannot be undone, and RelClause/PP attachments tie under class_lp. Those are the classic case for weighing all parses. Two other error clusters show that the categories feeding any chart are themselves unstable. First, term_high's verb/preposition confusion does not shrink with data: parse accuracy stays near 0.90–0.92 at 400, 900 and 1,500 sentences. Second, content-tree purity depends on training order. term_med with seed 17 reached purity 0.901 and omitted 42% of substructures, while seed 100 reached purity 0.990 and omitted 4.5% ([class_lp notes](/Users/karthiksing05/.claude/projects/-Users-karthiksing05-Documents-ISLE-Research-ChunkingCobweb/memory/project_class_lp_heuristic.md)).

Three audit findings constrain the v2 design. First, the shipped climbing-ancestor gate is vacuous. It passes as soon as the root's count exceeds 30, so the paper's graceful-failure halting never fires ([PAPER_CODE_AUDIT.md](../../trellis_v1/PAPER_CODE_AUDIT.md)). Memory tests found that even non-vacuous count gates did not separate constituents from non-constituents. Second, class_lp omits the −log P(i) normalization the paper describes. It is therefore a likelihood-weighted average of joint node scores, not a posterior ([cobweb_discrete_tree.cpp](../cobweb-private/src/cobweb_discrete_tree.cpp)). Third, before the C++ RNG determinism patch, identical configurations spread from 47% to 73% parse accuracy; the patch reduced that to ordinary seed variance, but it was reported inactive in the current environment ([determinism notes](/Users/karthiksing05/.claude/projects/-Users-karthiksing05-Documents-ISLE-Research-ChunkingCobweb/memory/project_cobweb_determinism.md)). A v2 chart must therefore bring its own pruning and calibrated potentials, and it must run on a Cobweb build that is verified to be deterministic.

## A Cobweb-scored chart with minimum-risk decoding makes the user's lattice exact

Inside-outside computes two totals for every span. The inside total is the weight of all ways to build the span; the outside total is the weight of all ways the rest of the sentence can surround it. Their normalized product is the posterior probability that the span is a chunk. Summed over a corpus, these posteriors are the expected counts that grammar re-estimation needs ([Lari & Young 1990](http://web.archive.org/web/20190112044228/https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse599d1/16sp/lari-young-90.pdf)). Eisner lists three uses for those expected counts: "(1) to train grammar weights from data, (2) to select low-risk parses, and (3) as soft features that characterize sentence positions". These map one-to-one onto v2's goals of unsupervised learning, decoding and chunk context. Eisner also shows that the outside quantities are adjoints of the inside computation, so one inside implementation produces both passes by backpropagation ([Eisner 2016](https://aclanthology.org/W16-5901/)).

INSIDE_OUTSIDE.md describes the algorithm as "computing the likelihood of each parse and 'freezing' / selecting the best one" ([INSIDE_OUTSIDE.md](../INSIDE_OUTSIDE.md)). That describes Viterbi decoding, which is a different decision rule. The user's lattice — keep a frontier of valid parses going up, then pick the best non-intersecting one going down — is an inside chart followed by a top-down minimum-risk decode. The first design decision is to keep two jobs separate: scoring and learning from marginals, and decoding.

Minimum-risk decoding is the formal version of "select the best non-intersecting set". Goodman showed that each decoder wins on the criterion it optimizes ([Goodman 1996](https://aclanthology.org/P96-1024/)). A parser judged on brackets should therefore maximize the expected number of correct brackets, not the probability of the whole tree. With latent-annotation grammars on WSJ, max-rule-product decoding scored 91.2 bracket F1, against 89.5 for the single best (Viterbi) derivation ([Petrov & Klein 2007](https://aclanthology.org/N07-1051/)). In TRELLIS notation, let φ be a non-negative potential for building label A over words i..j from children B and C. The quantities are:

```
inside    α(i,j,A)  = Σ_{k,B,C} φ(i,k,j,B,C→A) · α(i,k,B) · α(k+1,j,C)
outside   β(i,k,B) += β(i,j,A) · φ(i,k,j,B,C→A) · α(k+1,j,C)          (and symmetrically for C)
strength  μ(i,j)    = Σ_A α(i,j,A) · β(i,j,A) / Z
decode    M(i,j)    = max(0, μ(i,j) − θ) + max_k [ M(i,k) + M(k+1,j) ]   # θ = 0 gives a full binary tree
```

A complete binary tree always has n − 1 brackets. Maximizing expected correct brackets therefore minimizes expected omitted and expected spurious brackets at the same time. The threshold θ trades the two off, and it brings v1's "halt with a partial parse" behaviour inside the dynamic program. **At θ = 0.5 no dynamic program is needed at all.** Two crossing spans never appear in the same tree, so their posteriors sum to at most 1, and the set of spans with μ > 0.5 is always non-crossing. That set is a one-line extractor for chunks confirmed well enough to learn, which is the user's plan in INSIDE_OUTSIDE.md to "maintain candidate parses in a frontier and then learn them once we can confirm that they're good enough".

The same chart engine serves several purposes. Goodman's semiring framework covers recognition (a grammaticality judgement), counting how ambiguous a sentence is, computing marginals, finding the Viterbi parse, producing k-best lists, and computing expectations such as posterior entropy. The inside semiring needs only non-negative weights, so Cobweb scores can enter as Gibbs potentials exp(s/T) ([Goodman 1999](https://aclanthology.org/J99-4004/)). No published work characterizes how Cobweb posteriors behave in that role, so the temperature T must be swept and calibrated.

**The potentials matter more than the decoder,** and v1's supervised regime is already the best case in the classic literature. Gold unlabeled trees leave only the labels hidden. That is the fully bracketed setting, in which Pereira and Schabes found each training iteration linear in corpus length. In their experiments, training on bracketed text reached 90.36% bracketing accuracy, against 37.35% from raw text. Klein and Manning's Constituent-Context Model (CCM) is the closest prior model to TRELLIS. It scores every span by its yield (content) and its linear context, separately for constituents and for "distituents" (non-constituents), and it runs EM over binary bracketings. It reached 71.1 unlabeled F1 on WSJ10 ([Klein & Manning 2002](https://aclanthology.org/P02-1017/)), and 77.6 when combined with a dependency model ([Klein & Manning 2004](https://aclanthology.org/P04-1061/)). Its key move is a log-odds score: P(yield, context | constituent) / P(yield, context | distituent). A rare constituent also has a rare distituent profile, so log-odds does not penalize rarity. v1's raw sum does, and that rarity penalty is the documented reason the beam failed.

TRELLIS can implement the odds natively. Train distituent Cobweb trees on adjacent pairs of gold constituents whose union is not itself a gold constituent; these are exactly the "found the" errors. Then score each candidate as log P(x | tree⁺) − log P(x | tree⁻) using the existing `tree.log_prob`. Keeping this negative evidence in separate trees leaves the positive hierarchies, and therefore the generation pools, untouched. Strictly speaking, though, it adds trees, so it touches the "two hierarchies" pillar. The alternative is a visible constituent/distituent attribute inside the representation hierarchy. That keeps two trees but changes what generation draws from. The user should make that call.

The literature settles three more details about the chart's cells. First, cells should hold a few hard hypotheses rather than blended vectors. S-DIORA found that DIORA's soft averaging "is locally greedy and cannot recover from errors"; replacing it with a hard argmax and a per-cell beam improved unsupervised WSJ parsing by 2.2–6 F1 ([Drozdov et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.392/)). That suits Cobweb's discrete categorization, with the K top concepts kept per cell. Second, Cobweb labels act like the latent annotations in split-state PCFGs. Unlabeled brackets should therefore be decoded with labels summed out, rather than from the single best (span, concept) derivation ([Matsuzaki et al. 2005](https://aclanthology.org/P05-1010/); [Petrov & Klein 2007](https://aclanthology.org/N07-1051/)). Third, the chart only works if each item's score is local, and almost every v1 ingredient is. Child bags, complexity tags, the 5+5 word window, content-ref and the gate all depend only on the item. The one non-local ingredient is chunk-level context, the subject of the next section. Learning in the middle of a sentence would break the dynamic program, so the trees stay frozen for each sentence, as v1's evaluation already does.

The cost is manageable. A full chart over n words has n(n² − 1)/6 split points: 165 at n = 10 and 1,330 at n = 20, each multiplied by K² child-label pairs. v1's greedy loop needs only about 3n pair evaluations, but each one pays for two categorizations, six top-K bag encodings, up to six extra content-tree descents and four 200-node best-first passes, and class_lp reads only two of those passes ([parse_mh.py](../src/parse_mh.py)). Stripping the unused diagnostics comes first. After that, content sorts depend only on the two child labels and can be memoized across the corpus. Context sorts become O(n²) per sentence if content-ref is attached only to the decoded tree.

Because the shipped gate is vacuous, pruning has to come from elsewhere, and the literature offers two Cobweb-native sources. The first is coarse-to-fine parsing through nested category partitions. It cut parsing work tenfold with no loss of accuracy ([Charniak et al. 2006](https://aclanthology.org/N06-1022/)) and ran up to 100 times faster ([Petrov & Klein 2007](https://aclanthology.org/N07-1051/)). A Cobweb taxonomy cut at depth d is exactly such a partition. The second is closing chart cells using predicted constituent begin and end positions. That gave provable O(n²) and then linear worst-case parsing time with no loss of accuracy ([Roark & Hollingshead 2008](https://aclanthology.org/C08-1094/); [2009](https://aclanthology.org/N09-1073/)). BEGIN/END attributes on word context instances would let the representation hierarchy predict those positions. The first experiment should measure chart coverage: the fraction of gold spans that keep non-zero inside mass after gating. No decoder can recover a span the gate has closed.

The chart resolves the "stay greedy" rule rather than overriding it. An exhaustive whole-sentence chart is a computational-level idealization, not a plausible online mechanism. A memory store of three or four elements covers 97.66–99.96% of WSJ sentences under a right-corner transform ([Schuler et al. 2010](https://aclanthology.org/J10-1001/)). Incremental dynamic-programming beams run in linear time in practice, yet explore exponentially many trees by merging equivalent states ([Huang & Sagae 2010](https://aclanthology.org/P10-1110/)). The consistent division of labour has two parts. The chart is a slow teacher, used at learning and consolidation time. The online parser stays a fast easy-first student — the algorithm v1 already is ([Goldberg & Elhadad 2010](https://aclanthology.org/N10-1115/)) — trained with a dynamic oracle whose reference is the chart's MBR tree. Cross and Huang's provably optimal constituency oracle runs in amortized O(1) time ([Cross & Huang 2016](https://aclanthology.org/D16-1001/)), and LOLS lets a learned policy outperform an imperfect reference ([Chang et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02206)). This design also answers the "straying" objection raised against the earlier CKY. The chart adds no separately induced grammar: every item is a Cobweb categorization, and the chart only memoizes all merge orders at once.

One untested hypothesis about v1's beam deserves a check. A binary tree can be built in (n − 1)!/Π h(v) different merge orders, where h(v) is the number of internal nodes under v. A caterpillar tree has one order; a balanced eight-leaf tree has 80. If the beam did not merge identical frontiers, permutations of one partial tree could have crowded out genuine alternatives and biased the search toward balanced trees.

| Rank | Parsing option | What it buys | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cobweb-potential chart with MBR decoding | Chunk posteriors; exact lattice decode; partial parses via θ | O(n³K²) sorts; temperature calibration |
| 2 | CCM-style distituent trees for log-odds potentials | Removes the rarity penalty; explicit commission evidence for brackets | Extra trees (pillar question); how negatives are sampled |
| 3 | Chunk context from the outside pass, computed in a second pass | Chunk context without committing to neighbours | Approximate; roughly double the cost |

| 6 | Incremental dynamic-programming beam with ≤4 open chunks | Cognitively plausible online route; exact left chunk context | Repeats the beam's failure unless rank 2 lands first |

## Chunk context is the chart's outside pass, entered one granularity at a time

In v1, a composite's context is the surface words outside its span, not the neighbouring chunks. The optional `chunk_context` mode reads whatever frontier exists at the moment of a merge ([parse_mh.py](../src/parse_mh.py)). That frontier follows gold merge order in training and greedy order at test time. This is the mismatch behind INSIDE_OUTSIDE.md's remark that chunk context "is extremely hard to standardize in a parsing scheme that is inherently greedy" ([INSIDE_OUTSIDE.md](../INSIDE_OUTSIDE.md)).

The neural latent-tree literature shows how to get chunk context without that order dependence. In DIORA, the outside vector of a span "is a function of the outside vector of its parent ... and the inside vector of its sibling" ([Drozdov et al. 2019](https://aclanthology.org/N19-1116/)). Unrolled, that recursion gives the list of sibling chunks at every ancestor level, plus a root term: a "spine". The spine is a discrete reading of the user's "latents take all levels of content before and after". ReCAT stacks bottom-up and top-down contextual inside-outside layers for the same reason ([Hu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16319)). R2D2 predicts each word from its left and right "abstraction nodes" ([Hu et al. 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.379/)). The closest cognitive relative is HVM. It defines a variable as "distinct chunks sharing preceding and succeeding chunks", and it gets chunk context by parsing first and updating chunk-to-chunk statistics afterwards. HVM's sequence likelihood tracked human recall times at R = 0.86 in training and R = 0.70 in transfer ([Wu, Thalmann et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21332)).

Where chunk context should come from depends on the setting. In training on gold trees, every neighbouring chunk is known, so spine attributes can be built directly, HVM-style, with no new machinery. At parse time there are three options. First, a first chart pass with word-window context yields rule posteriors. From these, each span gets a posterior-weighted bag of sibling and parent concept ids, which a structural-context Cobweb tree scores to reweight a second pass. Second, the k-best trees can be reranked using exact neighbour concepts. Charniak and Johnson's 50-best lists contained trees scoring up to 96.8 ([Charniak & Johnson 2005](https://aclanthology.org/P05-1022/)), and reranking a packed parse forest reached 91.7 ([Huang 2008](https://aclanthology.org/P08-1067/)). Third, in an incremental parser, left context is exact because those chunks already sit on a stack of composed constituents, as in RNNGs ([Dyer et al. 2016](https://aclanthology.org/N16-1024/)), while right context stays at the word level. Kim, Dyer and Rush show one more way: global context can condition every potential through a per-sentence latent variable without breaking the dynamic program ([Kim, Dyer & Rush 2019](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1228/)).

**How chunk context enters matters as much as where it comes from,** and v1 has already run this experiment. Replacing word slots with neighbour concept ids at a maturity cut cut large-grammar omissions from 22% to 17%. On term_high, however, omissions rose from 21% to 52% ([faithful representation push](/Users/karthiksing05/.claude/projects/-Users-karthiksing05-Documents-ISLE-Research-ChunkingCobweb/memory/project_faithful_representation_push.md)). The sibling Cobweb-LLM project found that passing neighbours' concept paths to the next layer "hurt at this scale" ([METHODOLOGY_v1.md](../../Cobweb-LLM/experiments/v1/METHODOLOGY_v1.md)).

The Cobweb literature saw the same pattern earlier. Representing context words by their leaf concepts did "little better than chance". Representing them by the entire ancestor path, with counts, did best: it reached an adjusted Rand index near 1 on homonyms, against about 0.25 for raw words, because different words that share ancestors still match ([MacLellan, Matsakis & Langley 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11937)). Dependency parsers gained most from using coarse 4–6-bit cluster prefixes and full cluster strings together, which lifted English accuracy from 92.02% to 93.16% ([Koo et al. 2008](https://aclanthology.org/P08-1068/)). A child-language chunker also found word-level statistics more useful than 13 coarse classes ([McCauley & Christiansen 2011](https://csl-lab.psych.cornell.edu/files/2021/01/2011-mc-cogsci.pdf)).

The implementation that follows writes every reference to a learned concept at several levels of abstraction, each as its own attribute: the word or leaf, a coarse depth near part of speech, v1's depth 4, and the maturity cut. There is one bag per level per side. Category utility then chooses the informative level at each split. That turns v1's "helps dense grammars, hurts sparse lexicons" trade-off into something the learner chooses, which is also how v2 can keep the paper's one-parameter-set claim. Following S-DIORA, the bags should hold hard top-k concepts weighted by span posterior, never a soft blend over all candidate trees.

Long-range dependencies need a separate route, not a longer spine. Transformer Grammars found that forcing all context through recursively composed sentence vectors "harms perplexity on document-level language modeling" ([Sartran et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00633)). Pushdown Layers measure distance in closed constituents and gain 3–5× sample efficiency in syntactic generalization ([Murty et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19089)). The cognitive version is cue-based retrieval over a memory of chunks, with activation decay and interference between similar items ([Lewis & Vasishth 2005](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0000_25)). The minimal TRELLIS form takes the Cobweb-LLM "pair tree" and lifts it from words to chunks. That gives a dependency tree over (anchor chunk, earlier chunk, offset in closed chunks), whose concepts act as discrete heads. It would be a third hierarchy, so it should wait for the user's sign-off and for an agreement-style test that actually needs it.

Generation is where chunk context pays off most cheaply, and where the project's lock bites. v1's generator draws from a pool through a context-class filter, which amounts to parent annotation applied to sampling. Parent annotation is the cheapest large gain in treebank PCFGs: it raised labeled precision from 0.735 to 0.800 and recall from 0.697 to 0.792 on WSJ section 22 ([Johnson 1998](https://aclanthology.org/J98-4004/)). ADIOS argues that classes valid only inside their parent pattern are "inherently safer than grammars that posit globally valid categories" ([Solan et al. 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1187953)). The next step in that line is horizontal markovization, which also conditions each child on its already-generated left sibling ([Klein & Manning 2003](https://aclanthology.org/P03-1054/)). That context comes for free during top-down, left-to-right generation. Because generation is locked and judged qualitatively, this belongs in reserve. The lock does not shield generation from v2, however. The generator matches context leaves exactly, so any change to the representation hierarchy changes which pools a slot can draw from. Every representation experiment below therefore needs a qualitative re-check of generation, even with the generation code frozen.

## Bits make a chunk's value and the chunk count two terms of one sum

The user's touchstone is the right one. Goldsmith's Linguistica states the division of labour directly: "We develop a set of heuristics that rapidly develop a probabilistic morphological grammar, and use MDL as our primary tool to determine whether the modifications proposed by the heuristics will be adopted or not". The heuristics are needed because MDL "does not provide a set of heuristics that are nonetheless essential for obtaining candidate analyses" ([Goldsmith 2001](https://aclanthology.org/J01-2001/)).

**The pressure toward fewer units is not a separate count penalty; it comes from pointer costs inside the code.** Splitting a class used x + y times lengthens its pointers by (x + y)·H₂(x/(x+y)) bits. "Minimize the number of chunks" and "maximize chunk quality" are therefore two terms of one description length. Goldsmith also supplies the main caution: his MDL-driven triage wrongly removed 21.9% of correct changes, and he had to add an extra, non-MDL threshold. The user's reading of the paper — "likely grammars influence the proposition of similarly likely grammars" — matches Chen's trigger mechanism and Solomonoff's library-as-prior, both discussed below.

TRELLIS already has the architecture of this lineage; what it lacks is the lineage's shared code. Wolff's SNPR learned grammars by "hierarchical chunking" combined with "a search for disjunctive (part of speech) categories" ([Wolff 1982](http://web.archive.org/web/20221017150543/https://www.cognitionresearch.org/papers/ll/lang_comm_1982/wolff_1982.doc)). GRIDS reconstructed SNPR with a create operator, a merge operator, a description-length bias and a beam of three. Its authors proposed "an incremental version of Grids that processes only a few training sentences at a time" as the next step ([Langley & Stromsten 2000](http://www.isle.org/~langley/papers/grids.ecml2k.pdf)). Stolcke and Omohundro added a description-length prior with Dirichlet priors on the parameters. They found that a new chunk usually pays off only after several follow-up merges, which is why greedy best-first search failed and a beam of 3–10 was needed. Presenting samples in length order cut the work from 1,374 merges to 212 ([Stolcke & Omohundro 1994](https://arxiv.org/abs/cmp-lg/9409010)). Chen parsed each sentence once with the best grammar found so far. He considered only moves "triggered" in that parse and evaluated only the change in the objective. On an English-like artificial PCFG he reached 2.37 bits per word, against 2.46 for the best n-gram model and 2.60 for inside-outside ([Chen 1995](https://aclanthology.org/P95-1031/)). De Marcken added delete and undo moves, plus a significance test whose threshold "depends inversely upon the amount of evidence" ([de Marcken 1996](https://arxiv.org/abs/cmp-lg/9611002)). In these terms, a composition concept is GRIDS's create, a representation class is its merge, and a Cobweb split is Wolff's rebuild or Stolcke's unchunk. What TRELLIS lacks is a single codelength that adjudicates both kinds of move.

Proposals can stay cheap. Under GRIDS's code, the M best create-successors come from the M most frequent bigrams, an approach the authors call "totally equivalent to the exhaustive enumeration" ([Petasis et al. 2004](http://web.archive.org/web/20070710200647/http://www.iit.demokritos.gr:80/~paliourg/papers/GRAMMARS2004.pdf)). Clark showed that the description-length gain from chunking a pair equals the pair's pointwise mutual information ([Clark 2001](https://aclanthology.org/W01-0713/)). v1's frequency-ranked `MERGE_POLICY` is therefore a sound proposer, and chart posteriors summed over a corpus would be a better, soft one. Frequency also proposes frequent distituents, however. De Marcken showed that grouping by mutual information prefers [V P] over the correct [P N] whenever the verb–preposition bond is stronger ([de Marcken 1995](https://aclanthology.org/W95-0102/)). Acceptance therefore needs two tests: a description-length test, and a constituency filter. The filter can be either CCM's odds or Clark's criterion that "with real constituents, there is high mutual information between the symbol occurring before the putative constituent and the symbol after".

Acceptance can use one closed-form score. Adapt the Slim gain formula ([Smets & Vreeken 2012](https://vreeken.groups.cispa.de/pubs/2012/slim-smets,vreeken.pdf)) to two adjacent active concepts X and Y, where z is the number of times they occur together, x and y are their individual uses, and s is the number of tokens. The data saving from chunking them is then about z·[log₂(s·z/(x·y)) − log₂e] bits, minus the cost of defining the new symbol. This is my own derivation from Slim, not a quoted result. It says a chunk's value is (uses × bits saved per use) − (definition cost). Stitch's criterion has the same shape — "general enough that it applies in many locations, but specific enough that it captures a lot of structure at each location" ([Bowers et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16605)) — as does Minton's utility, savings × frequency − match cost ([Minton 1988](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1988/AAAI88-100.pdf)).

Three consequences follow. First, a pair must co-occur about e (≈2.7) times more often than chance — a PMI above 1.44 bits — before its definition cost even comes into play. Second, the threshold adapts: strongly bound pairs need few uses and weakly bound pairs need many. This is a threshold on information content, and Lan, Chemla and Katzir show that such thresholds behave differently from penalties on parameter magnitude. Under an MDL objective the correct aⁿbⁿ network became an optimum, which it was not under L1 or L2 penalties ([Lan, Chemla & Katzir 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.713)). Third, evaluating the gain at each pair of ancestors in the representation hierarchy and taking the maximum replaces the vacuous τ = 30 climb with an objective-driven "basic level for the pair".

A substitution gain adds one more term. It rewards candidate chunks whose contexts match an existing representation class, which makes it an MDL test of constituency. It rests on Clark and Eyraud's result that "it is sufficient to identify the syntactic congruence" ([Clark & Eyraud 2007](https://jmlr.org/papers/v8/clark07a.html)). It also counters the over-chunking of collocations that context-free unigram codes produce ([Goldwater, Griffiths & Johnson 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2009.03.008)). Commits should pass a significance margin, such as de Marcken's 1.96σ test or HVM's p = 0.05, and then stay on probation, with deletion if the gain later turns negative. These formulas are untested derivations. They need numerical checks against brute-force codelengths on the small grammars.

Incrementality comes from scoring with prequential codelength: the running sum of −log P(sentence_t | grammar learned from earlier sentences). For multinomial count models with Jeffreys (Krichevsky–Trofimov) smoothing, the prequential code equals the Bayesian marginal likelihood exactly ([Grünwald & Roos 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08484)). **A Cobweb node's contribution can therefore be computed from its current counts alone, independent of presentation order and without re-processing past data.** Algorithmic information theory explains why this preserves incremental additions. A sequential Bayes mixture's total excess prediction error is bounded by the prior codelength of the true source ([Rathmanner & Hutter 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.5721)), so structure is paid for once, through early mistakes. Solomonoff's incremental learner made each acquired concept part of the prior: "After we have solved the first problem, our a priori probability distribution changes. It includes the definition for F₁" ([Solomonoff 2003](http://raysolomonoff.com/publications/nips02.pdf)). A committed TRELLIS chunk becomes part of the reference language in the same way, and later chunks built from it are cheap to describe.

"Globally optimal" has to be defined within a restricted family of grammars. Finding the smallest grammar for a string is NP-hard, and unless P = NP no polynomial algorithm can come within a factor of 8569/8568 of the optimum ([Bannai et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06428)). Two results make this tolerable. First, any *irreducible* grammar is already an asymptotically optimal universal code ([Kieffer & Yang 2000](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.841160)). A grammar is irreducible if no adjacent pair repeats, every rule is used at least twice, and distinct rules expand to distinct strings. So "minimally viable" can be enforced with cheap local checks after each sentence. Second, greedy pair-merging comes within a constant factor of 0.333–0.625 of the optimal compression utility ([Kozma & Voderholzer, ESA 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08671)). That supports greedy acquisition, as long as the objective is compression utility.

On top of these results, the codelength-optimal cut through each Cobweb tree — the set of nodes that act as grammar symbols — can be computed exactly by a bottom-up dynamic program over the tree. Alternating between the two hierarchies cannot increase total codelength, so the alternation converges to a joint optimum that is exact within each hierarchy. A periodic "sleep" step that runs this cut along with the irreducibility, delete and merge passes is the tractable meaning of "globally optimal and minimally viable". Algorithmic statistics supports the user's stance that TRELLIS's grammar need not match the linguist's. The structure function selects the best model within a class "irrespective of whether the 'true' model is in the model class considered or not" ([Vereshchagin & Vitányi 2004](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0204037)). But intermediate grammars along a sequence of ever-shorter codes need not fit better ([Adriaans & Vitányi 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0612095)), so judge the final grammar rather than every step.

**One caveat decides whether MDL helps or hurts TRELLIS.** From positive data alone, MDL detects commission only if the data term is −log P(sentence | G) under the same normalized distribution that generates sentences. An over-general grammar then spreads probability onto non-sentences, so every real sentence costs more bits. v1's generator draws uniformly from a pool, applies an exact context-leaf filter, and falls back when the filter empties the pool. That procedure defines no coded distribution, so MDL computed over it cannot see commission. The project has already seen the result. met6's two-part MDL selection over content α cut categories from 16 to 5 and productions from 177 to 104, but generation grammaticality fell from 0.82 to 0.62, so commission rose from 18% to 38% ([threshold sweep notes](/Users/karthiksing05/.claude/projects/-Users-karthiksing05-Documents-ISLE-Research-ChunkingCobweb/memory/project_composite_threshold_sweep.md)). That is the failure the literature predicts from crude codes, small samples and misspecification. The Safe-Bayes remedy, a tempered likelihood with η < 1, targets the misspecification ([Grünwald & van Ommen 2017](https://doi.org/10.1214/17-ba1085)). There are two fixes, and both touch the generation lock. One is to define v1's generator as a normalized distribution without changing its behaviour. The other is to add explicit generation-based negative evidence: reject merges whose generated short strings are unattested ([indirect negative evidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15321)).

Unsupervised learning has to feed the parser's own analyses back into learning, because nothing else supplies structure. Chen, de Marcken, Brent and Stolcke all do this; the question is how to do it safely. The best-supported safeguard is to **store raw sentences rather than committed trees and re-parse them**, so a committed parse never becomes ground truth. Next comes forward (prequential) scoring, under which a chunk that only explains its own past parses loses bits. Undo and delete moves with hysteresis, soft or multiple parses early in learning, and probation for new candidates follow. A tempered likelihood and a short-sentences-first curriculum complete the set. Soft and hard updates each have a place. Viterbi training of a dependency model reached 44.8% on WSJ section 23, beating classic inside-outside ([Spitkovsky et al. 2010](https://aclanthology.org/W10-2902/)). Alternating soft and hard objectives (Lateen EM) added 5.5 points on average across 19 languages ([Spitkovsky, Alshawi & Jurafsky 2011](https://aclanthology.org/D11-1117/)). Stepwise online EM reached 65.4% part-of-speech accuracy after two passes, where batch EM reached 57.3% after 100 ([Liang & Klein 2009](https://aclanthology.org/N09-1069/)).

Online adaptor grammars already implement the user's frontier. After each minibatch they add candidate fragments, decay their statistics, and prune all but the top K by usefulness × a length bonus ([Zhai, Boyd-Graber & Cohen 2014](https://aclanthology.org/Q14-1036/)). One implementation blocker sits under every soft option: Cobweb's `increment_counts` hard-codes `count += 1` per instance ([cobweb_discrete_node.cpp](../cobweb-private/src/cobweb_discrete_node.cpp)). A posterior-weighted update therefore needs a weight argument in the C++ code. Without it, the alternative is to sample k trees from the chart and incorporate each one once.

| System | What sits in the "frontier" | Promotion test | Forgetting or undo |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Stolcke–Omohundro, incremental](http://web.archive.org/web/20070611231208/http://www.icsi.berkeley.edu/ftp/global/pub/ai/stolcke/thesis.ps.Z) | Rules specific to newly incorporated samples, 1–10 samples per batch | Merge or chunk while the description-length posterior improves | Unchunk and rechunk |
| [Chen 1995](https://aclanthology.org/P95-1031/) | Moves triggered by the current sentence's parse | Bayesian gain over all sentences so far | None |
| [ADIOS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1187953) | Candidate path bundles | Decrease ratio below η at significance α | None ("irreversibly rewired"); learners trained on different sentence orders are pooled instead |
| [Goldsmith 2001](https://aclanthology.org/J01-2001/) | Heuristic analyses | Adopt if and only if description length falls | Triage, plus an extra non-MDL threshold |
| [Online adaptor grammars](https://aclanthology.org/Q14-1036/) | Fragments sampled from the base distribution | Pitman–Yor reuse with decayed counts | Prune to the top K by usefulness × length |

The combination with the fewest hyperparameters is a significance floor plus a test that description length falls, with forgetting by decay. It would sit on a Cobweb-backed frontier that enters long-term memory only on promotion. That is the "virtual chunk" design in the project's archived formalization, which proposed that "we should add a new chunk **only when we have to**" ([FORMALIZATION.md](../src/archive/FORMALIZATION.md)). Compression is also a credible cognitive principle here, not just an engineering convenience: chunks behave like units of a maximally compressed code, with a span limit of about 3–4 chunks ([Mathy & Feldman 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2011.11.003)).

## Composition and representation hierarchies are a principled pair, not a rename

The reframing holds up formally. Every structure that unifies parts with kinds alternates two node types. And-Or graphs pair And-nodes, which decompose into components, with Or-nodes, which point "to alternative sub-configurations" ([Zhu & Mumford 2006](https://doi.org/10.1561/0600000018)). LearnSPN builds a product node when it can split the variables into independent groups, and a sum node when it clusters similar instances ([Gens & Domingos 2013](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/gens13.html)). Clark's syntactic concepts are closed pairs of a set of strings and a set of contexts ([Clark 2010](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~matilde/ClarkSyntacticConceptLattice.pdf)). "Primal" learners build nonterminals from strings and "dual" learners build them from contexts, and both approaches are legitimate ([Yoshinaka 2011, DLT](https://www.springerprofessional.de/towards-dual-approaches-for-learning-context-free-grammars-based/3686398)).

A Cobweb taxonomy is itself a hierarchy of mixture (sum) nodes over concepts that are products across attributes. The composition hierarchy is therefore a taxonomy over product-type objects, the chunks. The representation hierarchy is a taxonomy over contexts, and its concepts act as the Or-nodes: the substitution classes that fill composition slots. The commented-out paper text already says context does "the reciprocal work of representation" by crafting an identifier for each composite ([main.tex](../confs/acs-26/paper/main.tex)). The rename therefore re-describes v1's roles and probably does not touch the "two hierarchies" pillar, but the user should confirm. One limit remains. Clark's lattice lets a string belong to several concepts, while each Cobweb node has a single parent. v1's bag-of-concepts attributes are a pragmatic approximation of belonging to several concepts at once.

"Compose, then contextualize" also has a formal answer. In Clark's lattice, combining two concepts concatenates their strings and then *closes* the result through its contexts: composition produces a raw set of strings, and context decides what category it is. Johnson described a node's label as "a 'communication channel' that conveys information between the subtree dominated by the node and the part of the tree not dominated by this node" ([Johnson 1998](https://aclanthology.org/J98-4004/)).

For TRELLIS this means three passes. Pass A sorts the composition instance with no context at all. Pass B builds the representation instance from the element's own composition concept, its boundary words, its distance-weighted word window, the same window described by chunk concepts, and the spine. An optional pass C re-describes slots with pass-B concepts, as in the multi-pass labelling of MacLellan et al.'s Leaf system or ReCAT's stacked layers. Sorting during the passes must not modify the trees; commits happen only at the end. Here is an illustrative layout for the span "saw a dog" in "the cat saw a dog":

```
COMPOSITION instance (pass A, no context)      REPRESENTATION instance (pass B)
arity        : 2                                self.comp.d2 / .d4 : {C4} / {C45}
S1.word      : {saw: 1.0}                       edge.L.word        : {saw}     edge.R.word : {dog}
S1.rep.d2    : {R3: 1.0}      # ≈ verb class    ctx.L.word         : {cat: 1.0, the: 0.5}
S1.rep.d4    : {R31: .7, R34: .3}               ctx.L.rep.d2       : {R1: 1.0, R7: 0.5}
S2.rep.d2    : {R1: 1.0}      # ≈ NP class      ctx.L.chunk.d2     : {R1}      # "the cat" as one chunk
S2.comp.d3   : {C45: 1.0}                       spine.1            : (L, R1)   spine.2 : (-, ROOT)
S1/S2.cplx   : 0 / 1                            ctx.LR.pair.d2     : (R1|</s>) # Clark's left×right signal
seam.rep.d2  : (R3|R7)        # one conjunctive value                   role : constituent
```

Three further changes follow from the literature. The first is a model of distituents and of left-versus-right context dependence. Category utility treats attributes as independent within a concept, so it cannot see Clark's signal: whether the left and right contexts of a span are correlated. A single attribute that pairs left and right context at a coarse depth makes that signal visible. Context clusters separate categories far more easily than they separate constituents from distituents, which is why CCM needed an explicit distituent class ([Klein & Manning 2002](https://aclanthology.org/P02-1017/)). The quality of word classes matters too: run on words instead of gold part-of-speech tags, CCM fell from 70.14 to 57.29 EVALB F1 on held-out short PTB sentences ([Li et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.300.pdf)).

The second change is to propose compositions together with their slot classes, gated by a context-matrix test. Tu, Pavlovskaia and Zhu create a composition and its slot classes together as one "And-Or fragment" and accept it by posterior gain. The likelihood part of that gain factorizes into two coherence terms: one for the table of filler configurations (content), and one for the configuration × context matrix (context) ([Tu et al. 2013](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2013/hash/24681928425f5a9133504de568f5f6df-Abstract.html)). From Cobweb counts this becomes a concrete test. If a candidate's configuration × context table is close to rank one, its fillers are true substitution classes in that context. If not, the slot should be split locally, as ADIOS does, or the chunk rejected. This one test decides both "merge?" and "is this slot class real?". Neither question was settled by v1's vacuous gate or its intermediate nodes, which are only about 90% pure.

The third change is to reinstate boundary and seam features. Supervised span parsers are built on endpoint features. Stern et al. reached 91.79 PTB F1 by scoring spans from their endpoint states ([Stern, Andreas & Klein 2017](https://aclanthology.org/P17-1076/)). Kitaev and Klein found that "separating positional and content information in the encoder can lead to improved parsing accuracy" ([Kitaev & Klein 2018](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1249/)). v1 already defines a composite's context by its outermost positions. Boundary words therefore belong in the representation instance, and the seam belongs in the composition instance as one combined value at several levels of abstraction. **This is the boundary/seam feature set that was rejected as "hints" after it cut large-grammar omissions from 14% to 10%** ([representation push](/Users/karthiksing05/.claude/projects/-Users-karthiksing05-Documents-ISLE-Research-ChunkingCobweb/memory/project_acs26_f1_representation_push.md)). Reinstating it on theoretical grounds needs the user's approval.

Variable arity is best handled by keeping the binary parsing step and caching larger templates. Binary composition concepts keep the chart cheap. They also match SimplePCFG's factorization, in which the left and right children are independent given the parent, and that model reached 65.1 sentence-level F1 on PTB with 4,096 nonterminals ([Liu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14997)). The stored units can still be larger. On child-directed speech, restricting DOP fragments to depth one (a PCFG) dropped bracket F1 from 88.7 to 49.5 ([Bod 2009](http://web.archive.org/web/20180218230842/http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com:80/doi/10.1111/j.1551-6709.2009.01031.x/full)). Fragment grammars settle on mid-sized fragments with variables at their leaves ([O'Donnell, Tenenbaum & Goodman 2009](http://hdl.handle.net/1721.1/44963)). CHREST's templates have "a core that remains unchanged, and a set of slots, perhaps with default values" ([Gobet & Simon 1996, Cognitive Psychology](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/1339)).

The TRELLIS version promotes a consistently reused chain of binary composites to an n-ary template concept, with visible `arity`, `template` and `head` attributes. The same reuse or description-length test used to graduate chunks decides the promotion. The chain v1 once had to flatten to remove hollow VP trees is exactly such a case. Category utility will separate arities just as the visible complexity tag separated NP from S. The head attribute follows from RNNG analyses, which found that "headedness plays a central role in phrasal representation" ([Kuncoro et al. 2017](https://aclanthology.org/E17-1117/)). When evaluating against flatter n-ary gold trees, TRELLIS should emit a stored template as one n-ary node, because binarization alone caps PTB sentence-level F1 at 84.3 ([Kim, Dyer & Rush 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.10225)).

Relations beyond "before" let the same composition concept serve as both a merge rule and an idea. TRESTLE handles relational input with component and relational attributes such as `(On Component1 Component2)`. It aligns components to a concept by partial matching and then flattens them into dot-named attributes, so ordinary category utility applies ([MacLellan et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10588)). Partial matching is the costly part: it is the correspondence problem Labyrinth also faced ([Thompson & Langley 1989](https://mlanthology.org/icml/1989/thompson1989icml-incremental)). The cheaper route is a canonical slot order supplied by the domain, such as IDS operator slots, board coordinates or temporal order, with structure mapping as the fallback. Relation attributes between slots, such as `rel(s1,s2)=defends` or `rel(agent,S1,S2)`, then let one composition concept license both a syntactic merge and a semantic configuration. That realizes the user's "map the composition rules to both rules on deciding merging and deciding ideas". Zhu and Mumford's grammar of images uses horizontal links to carry spatial and functional relations in the same way.

## Chinese characters, chess and the Penn Treebank test different halves of the theory

Both founding documents name the next arena. Langley's essay describes a chess structure as "a spatial pattern of pieces that threaten or defend each other, but different types of pieces might occupy the same role" ([Langley 2025](http://www.cogsys.org/proceedings/2025/paper-2025-3.pdf)). The TRELLIS paper says three relevant things: "its composition operator is binary and its only relation is before", "Chess is the natural first target", and "treebanks offer a demanding test of unsupervised induction" ([Singaravadivelan & Langley 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.30414)).

Ordered by risk rather than by value, the domains run as follows. Chinese characters come first, as the smallest step to n-ary typed relations with gold trees. Chess comes second, because it needs the unsupervised chunk formation that v2 is building anyway. The Penn Treebank runs throughout as a staged benchmark. Hierarchical plans are a natural later arena. Langley's breakdown of HTN learning into structure, method heads and conditions maps onto chunks, concepts and context ([Langley 2025, AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/35091)), and the VAL plan validator is an exact commission oracle ([VAL](https://github.com/KCL-Planning/VAL)). But partial orders and variable binding make plans the hardest correspondence problem of all. Masked chunk prediction is not a separate domain; it is the training regime that serves all of them.

### Chinese-character grammars de-risk the n-ary, relation-typed step

Ideographic Description Sequences (IDS) are real, discrete, recursive 2D grammars. They are relation-labelled trees with no category labels, which is exactly v1's training regime. The cjkvi-ids file has 88,937 entries. Their recursive depth is mostly 2–5, and they include n-ary operators such as ⿲ and ⿳ ([cjkvi-ids](https://github.com/cjkvi/cjkvi-ids)). Arrangement matters: 呆 = ⿱口木 and 杏 = ⿱木口 have the same parts in different positions.

Human data exist both for legal novel items and for how characters are mentally represented. The Simplified Chinese Lexicon Project collected lexical decisions on all 8,105 standard characters and on 4,864 pseudocharacters generated from character structure, which gives a human-validated commission set ([Wang et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-025-02701-7)). Radical representations are position-specific ([Taft, Zhu & Peng 1999](https://doi.org/10.1006/jmla.1998.2625)), which predicts that the slot index must stay in the composition instance. Literate readers sort characters by configuration while novices sort them by components ([Yeh et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1080/13506280344000077)), which predicts that the composition taxonomy will reorganize as training proceeds.

Omission is the share of held-out real characters that fail to parse to a single root. Commission is the share of generated characters that contain an unattested (component, operator, slot) triple. Synthetic grammars built from the same operators give exact scoring first.

A replication of Orbán et al.'s grid scenes then tests chunk minimality sharply. People rate true shape combinations as familiar but rate the sub-pairs embedded in them only at chance. A Bayesian learner that keeps only "minimally sufficient" chunks fit their judgements at r = 0.88–0.92, against 0.74 and −0.23 for an associative learner ([Orbán et al. 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2268207)). A TRELLIS that builds triplets through recognizable intermediate pairs would wrongly predict that the sub-pairs feel familiar. Competitive exclusion — pruning chunks that better chunks subsume — restores the human pattern. In Zhu et al.'s unsupervised image grammar it cut 262 frequent compositions to 48 and was "the main factor that causes the bottom-up process to stop" ([Zhu et al. 2008](https://people.csail.mit.edu/leozhu/paper/usl_eccv_2008.pdf)).

The Jazz Harmony Treebank is a cheap one-dimensional sanity check. Its 150 expert binary trees have an average height of 7.57. Published combined span scores are 0.387–0.477 for an unsupervised neural PCFG and 0.623 for a supervised parser ([Harasim et al. 2020](https://github.com/DCMLab/JazzHarmonyTreebank); [Cartuyvels et al. 2024](https://bnaic2024.sites.uu.nl/wp-content/uploads/sites/986/2024/11/Unsupervised-Induction-of-Harmonic-Syntax.pdf)).

### Chess recall already scores reconstruction in omission and commission

Chess is the flagship domain because its human literature already uses TRELLIS's vocabulary. Gobet and Simon score position recall in three ways: % correct; errors of omission, "the number of pieces in the stimulus position minus the number of pieces placed"; and errors of commission, "pieces placed incorrectly". They report all three for humans and for CHREST, the leading symbolic chunking model ([Gobet & Simon 2000](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/811/1/Five%20Seconds%20or%20Sixty%20.pdf)).

**CHREST fits omissions well but not commissions.** It underestimates Class A players' commission errors and overestimates those of experts and masters, because "the image of a chunk may contain information about the locations of more pieces than just those used to recognize the pattern". With a 10-second presentation, a 300,000-node CHREST net recalled 64.5% of a position without template slots and 84% with them, against 85–96% for human masters. A Cobweb concept is a natural probabilistic template: low-entropy attributes form the core, high-entropy attributes form the slots, and modal values give the defaults. If such templates predicted commission errors better than CHREST, that would be a crisp cognitive-modelling result.

| Human mean errors per game position (Gobet & Simon 2000) | Masters | Experts | Class A |
|---|---|---|---|
| Omission | 0.6 | 5.8 | 9.5 |
| Commission | 2.2 | 4.2 | 4.5 |

Chase and Simon supply both the relation inventory and the chunk statistics. Their relations were attack, defense, proximity, common colour and common type. On the first middle-game trial, the master recalled 7.7 chunks averaging 2.5 pieces each, and 47 of the master's 77 chunks were pawn chains. Placing a prototype master position from memory alone already scores "better than 44%", which gives a baseline for generation that uses only prior knowledge ([Chase & Simon 1973](http://matt.colorado.edu/teaching/highcog/fall8/cs73.pdf); [Simon & Chase 1973](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/chess/1973-simon.pdf)).

Data are abundant. Lichess publishes about 8.22 billion games and 6.16 million motif-tagged puzzles under CC0 ([Lichess](https://database.lichess.org/)). python-chess computes the typed relations (attacks, attackers, pins). It also provides a legality check, but only as a necessary condition; its documentation warns that "reachability is not guaranteed" ([python-chess](https://python-chess.readthedocs.io/en/latest/core.html)).

Four representation choices follow. A primitive is a piece on a square. Relations are typed edges between pieces. Candidate chunks are restricted to connected subgraphs of the relation graph; without that restriction, a 25-piece position has exponentially many candidate subsets. Location is coded as an absolute anchor square plus relative offsets, so that Cobweb's attribute distributions decide whether a chunk is tied to a location. Humans recall mirrored positions less well, which implies location-specific chunks ([Gobet & Simon 1996, Memory & Cognition](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8757497/)).

Out-of-distribution tests come for free: mirrored and translated positions, recalling several boards at once, and Chess960. In Chess960, a 270M-parameter chess transformer's recognition of chess concepts drops 10–20% ([Lomasov et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26025)). Chess has no gold parse trees, so it depends on v2's unsupervised chunking. Proxy segmentations, such as connected components of the relation graph, should serve only as diagnostics, never as training targets. Pins and x-rays are discontinuous, so chess eventually needs chunks with fan-out 2 (two separate pieces of the board) and a co-indexing relation. That is the same machinery cross-serial languages require ([Yoshinaka 2011, TCS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2010.12.058)).

### The Penn Treebank rewards protocol discipline more than clever search

The standard full-WSJ protocol trains on sections 02–21, tunes on section 22 and tests on section 23. It removes punctuation, lowercases everything, caps the vocabulary at 10K words, and scores sentence-level F1 with trivial spans discarded. Under this protocol right-branching trees score 39.5, the ceiling for binarized gold trees is 84.3, and the compound PCFG scores 55.2 (60.1 at best). A plain count-based PCFG trained by EM scored below 35 — worse than right-branching — "despite a thorough hyperparameter search" ([Kim, Dyer & Rush 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.10225)).

The best PTB-only grammar inducers sit at 64–65: SimplePCFG at 65.1 ± 2.1 ([Liu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14997)) and the holographic PCFG at 64.6 ± 0.4 ([Yamaki et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08063)). Systems near 70 borrow structure from other parsers: parse-focusing reaches 69.6 ± 0.6 ([Park & Kim 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16181)) and tree-averaging ensembles reach 70.4–72.8 ([Shayegh et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01717)). Others draw on pretrained language models.

The short-sentence WSJ10 track gives gold part-of-speech tags as input and tests on the training sentences. There CCM scores 71.9, DMV+CCM 77.6 and right-branching 61.7, against an upper bound of 88.1 ([Klein & Manning 2004](https://aclanthology.org/P04-1061/)). UML-DOP reports 82.9, though the papers describe its binarization of gold trees ambiguously ([Bod 2006](https://aclanthology.org/P06-1109/)). The incremental, greedy CCL parser reaches 75.9 on WSJ10 from raw words, but it uses punctuation cues ([Seginer 2007](https://aclanthology.org/P07-1049/)). On held-out section 23 at all lengths, Ponvert's cascaded chunkers — the nearest relative of TRELLIS's merge-and-replace parse — score 54.2 against CCL's 51.7 ([Ponvert, Baldridge & Erk 2011](https://aclanthology.org/P11-1108/)). Incremental induction is itself rare: a systematic review found only 1 of 33 implementations processing input incrementally ([Muralidaran, Spasić & Knight](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/natural-language-engineering/article/systematic-review-of-unsupervised-approaches-to-grammar-induction/413BE0F463829B4C23DCB8EBD67B4DFF)).

TRELLIS's machinery maps onto components that have already worked elsewhere. Its left and right child-class attributes are SimplePCFG nonterminals. Its representation hierarchy is a hierarchical, incremental CCM that still lacks a distituent model. Its climbing-ancestor walk is the kind of back-off sharing that lifted count-based PCFGs from below 35 to 50.8, provided the walk is used as a probability mixture over ancestors rather than as a yes/no gate. Extrapolating from these neighbours gives rough expectations on the full-WSJ metric. A naive unsupervised v2 should land near 35–45. With hierarchy back-off, a distituent filter, soft frontier evidence and ensembles across data orders, the 45–58 band spanned by CCL and the compound PCFG is plausible. Scores above 60 have so far required thousands of symbols, external teachers or signal from pretrained LMs. Supervision does not break this ceiling either: a 30-nonterminal model focused on gold trees reached only 73.7 ([Park & Kim 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20734)). These ranges are extrapolations, not measurements, because no prior system combines Cobweb-style concept formation with span posteriors.

Protocol discipline matters more than any model choice. Metric variants applied to the same parses can differ by more than 20 points ([Li et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.300.pdf)). Reproductions of published systems come in 3–7 points below the reported numbers ([Park & Kim 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16181)). Tuning on as few as 15 labelled trees beats unsupervised model selection ([Shi, Livescu & Gimpel 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.614.pdf)). The plan should therefore start with the evaluator. TRELLIS should reimplement the conventions of the compound-PCFG `eval.py` ([eval.py](https://github.com/harvardnlp/compound-pcfg/blob/master/eval.py)) and reproduce right-branching at 39.5 and the oracle at 84.3 before scoring itself. It should report omission (1 − span recall) and commission (1 − span precision) at sentence and corpus level, by length bucket, per gold label, and across different orderings of the data stream. Where a table must sit beside published numbers, add their harmonic mean in a column labelled with the papers' own name for it. Hyperparameters should be chosen by description length or held-out likelihood, not by section-22 brackets.

Two further measures belong alongside these. One is a non-crossing-bracket diagnostic, which separates "different but compatible binarization" from real attachment errors. It speaks directly to a met6 finding: an unsupervised learner on the medium grammar matched only 46% of gold substructures (against 84.6% for the supervised learner of that era) while generating 98.3% grammatical sentences ([threshold sweep notes](/Users/karthiksing05/.claude/projects/-Users-karthiksing05-Documents-ISLE-Research-ChunkingCobweb/memory/project_composite_threshold_sweep.md)). The other is language-level omission and commission: the share of section 23 covered by a parse, and the grammaticality of samples on minimal-pair suites. Full PTB requires an LDC Treebank-3 licence ([LDC99T42](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC99T42)). The free NLTK sample of 3,914 WSJ trees ([NLTK](https://www.nltk.org/book/ch08.html)) is enough for development, but its numbers must never be compared with section 23.

### Masked chunk prediction unifies training, scoring and diffusion

For TRELLIS, masked modelling and diffusion are one regime. Masked diffusion models are formally masked models in which the time variable plays no role ([Zheng et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02908)), and their loss reduces to a mixture of masked-language-model losses ([Sahoo et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07524)). Category utility is itself an expected gain in correctly predicting attribute values ([Fisher 1987](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00114265)). Cobweb's pattern completion is therefore already the denoiser of a discrete diffusion process that masks attribute slots. Cobweb/4L also learns masked word prediction faster than transformers when data are scarce ([Lian, Wang & MacLellan 2025](https://tail.cc.gatech.edu/publications/lian-csr-2025)).

The proposed regime is outside-masked chunk prediction. It hides whole chunks at each level. It then predicts the hidden chunk's basic-level composition concept from its context in the representation hierarchy, and predicts its parts from that concept in the composition hierarchy. The resulting score does three jobs: it is the outside × inside score for chart items, the promotion test that moves frontier chunks into memory, and a held-out evaluation number. The unit of masking matters. Masking whole statistically cohesive units beats masking random tokens, because masking one part while its collocated partner stays visible lets a model "latch onto shallow local signals" ([Levine et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.01825)). SpanBERT's boundary objective, which predicts span content from the tokens just outside it, is the same idea as DIORA's outside reconstruction ([Joshi et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.tacl-1.5/)).

"Chunk the context window" has a concrete answer. At each level, the window should have a constant width measured in that level's chunks. It holds about two or three same-level neighbours as basic-level concept ids, plus each neighbour's boundary parts (information carried up from the level below), plus slots for the parent and siblings outside the span, plus one coarser slot. Inputs should sit at the basic-level cut of the current level and the one below it. The target should be the hidden chunk's basic-level concept first, then its parts.

Theory explains why chunk units work. Diffusion models learn PCFG-like data "with the sample complexity required for clustering features with statistically similar context". This clustering "emerges hierarchically: higher-level features associated with longer contexts require more data" ([Favero et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12089); [Cagnetta & Wyart 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00048)). Writing each level's contexts in units already clustered at the level below shrinks the correlation range that must be learned. It also argues for learning the levels in order.

The same picture makes the user's diffusion intuition precise. Replacing words with their ancestor clusters before masking beat plain masking, with the best number of clusters near the square root of the vocabulary size ([Zhou et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08632)). On this view, parsing is the forward process, which coarsens, and top-down generation is the reverse. The user's "noise two tokens at a time" is coarsening in the same direction as BPE. Unmasking the easiest positions first lifted a masked model's Sudoku accuracy from under 7% to about 90% ([Kim et al. 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kim25ah.html)). That is the diffusion-side argument for posterior-guided easy-first decoding as the default approximation to a full chart.

Four cautions carry over. First, masked variants must be used only for prediction and scoring, never fed back into Cobweb's counts. Second, much of masked diffusion's data-efficiency advantage in neural networks comes from masking acting as regularization ([Gao et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04071)). A count-based learner does not need that, so the value that transfers is the many partial-instance prediction problems each sentence provides. Third, implicit chunk structure in a hierarchical RNN was "transient" when nothing in the objective rewarded it ([Wu, Deshmukh et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04919)), so chunk-level masking should stay in the training regime permanently. Fourth, the winning BabyLM 2024 system mixed causal and masked objectives at about 1:7 ([BabyLM 2024 findings](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.05149)). That suggests mostly bidirectional chunk masking, with a minority of left-to-right prediction to keep incremental processing honest.

One housekeeping item also needs fixing. The paper's `diffusion-grammar` bibliography entry lists an anonymous paper titled "Diffusion models for unsupervised sentence parsing through chunk construction" at arXiv:2502.12089. That ID belongs to Favero et al.'s differently titled paper, and no paper with the listed title could be found ([main.bib](../confs/acs-26/paper/main.bib)). The entry should be corrected before any v2 write-up cites it.

## Conclusion

The user's notes rank inside-outside as "nice to have" and file unsupervised chunk aggregation, better representations and new domains under "NEED". The literature reverses that ordering in terms of dependency, though not importance. The chart's posteriors are the frontier evidence that unsupervised aggregation consumes. Its outside pass is the only source of chunk context that does not depend on merge order. Its marginals are the "strength" that should replace a count gate the audit found vacuous. Inside-outside is therefore the substrate for all three needs, but only once the potentials change. That is the lesson v1 already taught: every gain came from representation, and every search change lost. The 1990s taught it too. Raw inside-outside converged to 300 different local minima from 300 starting points ([Carroll & Charniak 1992](https://cdn.aaai.org/Workshops/1992/WS-92-01/WS92-01-001.pdf)), and that changed only when CCM changed what a span is scored by.

The synthesis also shows where TRELLIS v2 would be new. No published system combines its pieces: an incremental, hierarchical CCM whose operators are SNPR/GRIDS's create and merge, run over two Cobweb taxonomies and governed by one prequential bits objective computed from Cobweb's own counts. Each of its nearest relatives lacks at least one piece. CCM is batch and flat, online adaptor grammars have no context taxonomy, HVM has neither a taxonomy over chunks nor an evaluation of generation, and CHREST has neither relations nor context. That leaves three results that would be distinctly TRELLIS's own. The first is fitting human chess commission errors where CHREST fails. The second is an incremental learner on PTB, in a field where 1 of 33 implementations is incremental. The third is showing that the generation lock and an MDL objective are compatible only if v1's pool-plus-context-class-filter generator is defined as a normalized distribution. That last decision should come first, because it determines whether bits can see commission at all.
